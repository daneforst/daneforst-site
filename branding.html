Identity

The smallest thing a brand owns, and the hardest working.

DisciplineLogo & Identity Design
FocusMarks, Wordmarks & Systems
StudioDEF Studios, Portland OR

Anyone can make a logo. A mark is the smallest thing a brand owns and the one that works the hardest: on a bottle, on a sign, stitched into a hat, etched into glass, and twelve pixels wide in somebody’s browser tab. These are the ones I keep coming back to.

Simple on purpose

A mark earns its keep by surviving. Shrunk to a favicon, blown up on a building, embroidered, foiled, sandblasted, or faxed by somebody’s uncle. If it only works at full size in full color, it is an illustration, not an identity.

A mark, then a system

The logo is the door. Behind it sit the color, the type, the patterns, the photography, and the rules for how they behave together. That is the part that makes a brand feel like one thing across a shelf, a site, a truck, and a business card.

So most of these did not stop at a mark. They kept going into palettes, secondary marks, patterns, and the packaging and web work that carries them.

Made to be used

Every mark leaves here as real files. Vector originals, one-color and reversed versions, clear-space and minimum-size rules, and the formats your printer, your sign shop, and your developer will actually ask for. No surprises six months later.

How deep does your brand go?

Anyone can make a logo. I am interested in building the stories, symbols, worlds, and experiences people carry with them. If that is the kind of thing you are after, we should talk.
