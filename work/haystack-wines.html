03 / 08 — Brand Identity + Packaging

Haystack
Wines

coastal character in every detail

RoleBrand Identity / Packaging

Haystack Wines was one of several brands developed under the Mellor Ranch Farms umbrella in California. While many wine projects lean heavily on tradition, ornamentation, and familiar visual tropes, Haystack presented an opportunity to create something more restrained — a brand built around a single strong idea rather than layers of decoration.

How did this project begin?

Haystack Wines was part of a larger relationship we had developed with Greg Mellor and Mellor Ranch Wines. Through previous work in the California wine industry, we were brought in to help shape several distinct labels under the Mellor Ranch umbrella. Haystack was one of those brands.

Alongside Woollie, Rabbit Hole, and several others, the goal was to create identities that felt completely different from one another while still existing comfortably under the same parent company.

What was the challenge?

The challenge was creating an identity that could stand on its own among a growing family of brands. Mellor Ranch wanted each label to have its own personality — a world their devotees could enter and be enveloped in.

They had previously worked with another label designer and were getting results that felt expected, safe, and honestly a little boring. How do we create something rooted in agriculture without falling into every agricultural cliché? When they go left, we go right.

What was your approach?

The creative thesis behind Haystack was actually pretty simple: Go against the grain. Pun intended.

Most of the directions we were seeing felt overly rustic, overly rugged, and a little too literal. Instead, we wanted something cleaner. More refined and modern. The goal wasn’t to remove the agricultural influence — it was to make it more subtle.

The logo became the hero. The final solution incorporated a stylized H and a pair of pitchforks in a way that felt more discovered than announced. Symbols that reward people for looking closer.

What was your role?

I was responsible for the logo design, label design, visual identity, typography, color direction, photography direction, and overall branding system.

One of the hidden challenges of wine branding is that a surprising amount of work happens before you ever start designing — naming conventions, trademark issues, legal considerations, and existing brand conflicts all influence the final outcome. There was a lot of research and problem solving before we ever put pen to paper.

How did you know it was working?

Haystack was one of those rare projects where the solution arrived surprisingly quickly. Not because it was easy — we just instinctually knew exactly what we weren’t trying to make. The client had already shown us examples of directions that weren’t working, which gave us clarity from the start.

The logo landed almost immediately. It became the first proof point that this family of brands could achieve distinct personalities while still maintaining a cohesive level of quality. It could simply exist on the bottle and do its job. For a logo designer, that’s the sweet spot.

Why does this project matter?

Haystack remains one of the most refined pieces in my portfolio. While many of my projects showcase illustration, storytelling, or larger experiential systems, this one demonstrates restraint.

Designers spend their entire careers chasing that balance between simplicity and meaning in a logo. The challenge is knowing what to remove until the essence stands on its own. Haystack earns your consideration.
